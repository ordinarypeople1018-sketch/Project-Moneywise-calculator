How to Ask for a Salary Increase (and Actually Get It)

6 min read · MONEYWISE editorial

Understand what you are paid for

Employers pay for the value you create and for the cost of replacing you. Both are negotiable facts, not feelings. Before any conversation, gather market data for your role, level and location from multiple sources.

Build the evidence file

Maintain a running document of what you delivered and the measurable impact — revenue generated, costs saved, projects shipped, problems solved. Review it before your review meeting. Memory is unreliable; the document is not.

Choose the right moment

The best moments are after a visible win, during a scheduled review, or when a new responsibility is being assigned. The worst moment is during a company-wide cost-cutting cycle.

Ask for a specific number

"I would like a raise" invites a small gesture. "Based on market data and my results, I am looking for a base of $X" invites a real conversation. Anchor with a defensible number, slightly above your actual target.

Be ready to hear no

If the answer is no, ask what specifically would need to be true for a yes in six months, and get it written down. A documented path is worth more than a vague promise.
