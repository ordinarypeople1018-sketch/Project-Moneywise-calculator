Budgeting

How to Build a Budget That Actually Works

6 min read · MONEYWISE editorial

Start with what you actually spend

The biggest mistake in budgeting is starting from what you think you spend. Most people underestimate discretionary spending by 20–30%. Before you set a single target, look at the last two months of bank statements and categorise every transaction. It is uncomfortable and it is the single most valuable step.

Separate fixed from variable

Fixed costs — rent, insurance, loan payments, subscriptions — are decided once and then happen automatically. Variable costs — food, transport, entertainment — are decided daily. These need completely different treatment. Fixed costs should be optimised once a year. Variable costs need an ongoing system.

Give variable spending a weekly number

A monthly food budget of $500 is hard to act on. A weekly budget of $115 is something you can check on a Sunday. Weekly cycles match how people actually make decisions, and they catch overspending before it becomes a crisis.

Budget for irregular expenses

Car servicing, dental work, gifts, annual subscriptions — these are predictable but infrequent. Divide the annual total by 12 and set that aside monthly. This single habit eliminates most "unexpected" expenses.

Review, do not restart

A budget that fails in month one is not a failure — it is data. Adjust the number and continue. The people who succeed at budgeting are not the ones with perfect numbers; they are the ones who keep coming back.
